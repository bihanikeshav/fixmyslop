# Distinctiveness: Personal & idiosyncratic sites

> These are the proof that personality comes from mining the subject's own world — every enduring signature move traces back to a specific person's specific obsession, executed with craft.

---

## Exemplars

### Lynn Fisher — lynnandtonic.com

- **Signature:** A complete, from-scratch CSS redesign every year — not a retheme but a wholly new concept. Each year exploits a CSS feature that barely existed when she started. The 2017 redesign uses 21 distinct layouts revealed as you resize the browser (Kuleshov Effect applied to viewport width). The 2020 redesign uses pop-up-book folding panels, draggable pull-tabs with dissolve reveals, and a sprite-based avatar that changes costume at each media query breakpoint. The 2022 redesign lets you drag video-game power-ups (mushrooms, potions) into project cards, which then grow via container queries and change color via CSS variables — state persisted in localStorage.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Constraint-as-concept* — pick a single, freshly-landed CSS feature and build an entire annual concept around demonstrating it experientially, not just using it. Also: *breakpoint as animation* — responsive resizing becomes the interaction itself, not just a layout fallback.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** The annual cadence as a ten-year public commitment, the specific game-mechanic metaphors, the sprite costume system.

### Devine Lu Linvega — wiki.xxiivv.com

- **Signature:** The entire site is generated by Oscean, a custom wiki engine written in C and later in Uxntal (the assembly language for Linvega's own Uxn virtual machine). The visual language is strictly monochrome with one aqua accent — no photographs except documentary images from life aboard their sailboat *Pino*. Navigation is a flat hierarchy of wiki-linked nodes organized into audio / visual / research, with orphan and dead-end tracking built into the system itself. Every software tool on the site was built by Linvega: Uxn, Left (text editor), Noodle (paint program), Orca (music sequencer). The site footer is badged "Uxn Powered," making the toolchain the signature.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Hand-built world* — build your site in tools you also built; the site becomes evidence of the practice. *Constraint palette* — black/white/one-accent is not minimalism for aesthetics; it is a philosophical position (permacomputing, anti-consumption). *The site is the log* — a living document of a nomadic practice rather than a portfolio.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** The Uxn VM, the sailboat lifestyle, the Merveilles community aesthetic, the specific NoNazis + CreativeCommons iconography footer.

### Bruno Simon — bruno-simon.com

- **Signature:** The entire portfolio is a drivable 3D scene — you navigate by steering a remote-controlled toy car through a low-poly world. No traditional nav menus exist; projects are discovered by bumping into objects. Physics (Cannon.js) and 3D rendering (Three.js) are combined so the car knocks over objects and triggers reveals. The visual style — tiny rounded architecture, flat colors, baked shadow PNGs — comes from a specific reference: Micro Machines on PlayStation. The whole site weighs 2.8 MB. Awwwards Site of the Day; 160,000 visits in first week.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Personal metaphor as the navigation system* — a childhood toy or interest becomes the literal UI, removing all conventional chrome. *Environment as instruction* — tiles create paths, walls create boundaries; no tutorial needed because the world teaches you how to move through it.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** The RC car specifically, the Micro Machines aesthetic, the particular Three.js + Cannon.js implementation (widely cloned; clones read as derivative immediately).

### Cassie Evans — cassie.codes

- **Signature:** An SVG self-portrait ("lil me") on the homepage reacts to the visitor's cursor — the illustrated face tracks pointer position using GSAP, creating faux-3D parallax by moving foreground elements faster than background ones. The quote that defines the philosophy is explicit: "Exporting animation as an mp4 is cool and all. But you can't *play* with it." Every tutorial and talk is presented as a live demonstration, not a screenshot. The illustration is her own likeness, making the interactive element literally a stand-in for her presence on the page.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Self-portrait as interactive element* — a hand-drawn character that responds to the visitor's cursor presence collapses the distance between author and reader. *Playability as value* — the site's own UI demonstrates what the creator teaches: animation on the web is participatory, not broadcast.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** The specific lil-me SVG illustration, the GSAP fairy-codemother identity, Cassie's likeness.

### Maggie Appleton — maggieappleton.com

- **Signature:** Content is classified by horticultural growth stage — 🌱 Seedlings (rough notes), 🌿 Budding (developing), 🌳 Evergreen (mature). Every note shows "planted" and "last tended" timestamps, making intellectual cultivation visible. Navigation abandons reverse-chronological feeds entirely in favor of contextual backlinks and associative clusters (essays, notes, patterns, library, talks). Hover-preview tooltips (Tippy.js) let you read linked notes without leaving the current page. Custom illustrations and diagrams appear throughout as first-class content — not decoration.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Metaphor made structural* — the garden metaphor is not decorative copy; it reorganizes the entire information architecture, replacing "date published" with "stage of growth." *Epistemic transparency as UI* — showing confidence levels and cultivation timestamps turns the site into a record of thinking-in-progress, not finished authority.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** The specific growth-stage emoji taxonomy, the "digital gardening" framing Maggie has become the primary proponent of, the visual essay illustrations.

### Sara Soueidan — sarasoueidan.com

- **Signature:** Custom SVG horizontal rules across the site: hand-selected bird silhouettes perched on a curvy wire, implemented as inline SVG (not background-image) with ARIA semantics so they are announced correctly by screen readers, remain visible in Windows High Contrast Mode, and adapt to dark/light themes. A deliberate light/dark mode toggle explicitly labels each state ("Light" / "Dark") rather than using an ambiguous icon — because Soueidan's entire practice is about accessibility-first implementation, and the toggle is itself a demonstration. The site is being rebuilt in public on sarasoueidan.dev, documented with a process blog post.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Expertise made visible in the details* — the most mundane element (a horizontal rule) becomes a demonstration of the creator's domain. *Decorative element that teaches* — the birds-on-a-wire trick is both personality and an implicit tutorial in accessible SVG decoration.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** The specific bird SVG illustration, Soueidan's accessibility-engineer identity as the explanatory frame.

### Tom MacWright — macwright.com

- **Signature:** The site maintains five parallel content streams with equal visual weight: Writing, Reading, Photos, Projects, Drawings. A dedicated `/reading` section stores every book finished since at least 2017 — moved off Goodreads, with individual reviews, organized as a personal canon rather than a log. A monthly "Recently" post enforces publication cadence. The design has barely changed in years — MacWright explicitly says he is "proud of how little the website has changed" and sells the theme for $100 because people keep asking for it. Light/Dark/Auto toggle is present but the typographic spareness is the identity.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Stability as personality* — resisting redesign makes the accumulating archive the statement. *Canon-over-stream* — organizing reading as a personal library (not a feed) signals a different relationship with time and attention than a blog. *Multi-channel identity with equal weight* — showing five different kinds of making at the same visual priority refuses to collapse the person into one role.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** MacWright's specific "Recently" format, the particular blend of mapping/data work and drawing/photography, the minimal typographic style.

### Anand Chowdhary — anandchowdhary.com

- **Signature:** The homepage is a real-time life dashboard: current step count pulled from a fitness tracker, calorie intake and macro breakdown, live sleep data from Oura and Apple devices, what song is currently playing, location in time zones across a ten-year travel history covering 17 countries. Every section has an API. A `/versions` page documents each iteration of the site's design going back to the first version. A yearly theme ("Year of Discovery" for 2026) and birthday countdown give the site ritualistic, calendar-driven personality. The site doubles as a personal data open API.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Live self-data as content* — instead of a static bio, the site becomes a real-time state of the person. *Versioning as narrative* — a public archive of past designs makes the site's own evolution part of its story. *Ritual rhythm* — annual themes and countdowns give a personal site the feeling of a living calendar.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** Anand's specific biometric integrations, the particular "Year of X" naming convention, the exact multi-identity framing (GitHub Star, entrepreneur, advisor, investor simultaneously).

### Robb Knight — rknight.me

- **Signature:** The site is an IndieWeb accumulator of obsessive logs: Almanac (every film, TV show, book, album tracked across its third or fourth rebuild), a `/now` page auto-updated by pulling Mastodon posts, an "App Defaults" project that grew into a community site with 300+ responses, and a digital garden (Intersect) that migrated onto the main domain rather than living separately. Nearly 50 DNS subdomains. EchoFeed (a side project that syncs content between platforms) lives as a named product alongside the personal site. The sticker merchant identity co-exists with software authorship and blogging with no hierarchy.
- **Transferable technique (the move):** *Obsessive log as site personality* — exhaustive self-documentation of consumption and tools makes the person visible through accumulation rather than a crafted bio. *Product and person as one domain* — shipping side products at subdomains of a personal domain makes the maker's commercial and personal identity inseparable.
- **Don't copy (it's theirs):** Robb's specific Almanac stack, the EchoFeed product, the sticker merchant branding, the particular combination of media obsession and Eleventy/IndieWeb nerdery.

## Category pattern

Distinctiveness in personal and idiosyncratic sites never comes from visual style first — it comes from a specific person's world made interactive. Every signature move in this category is traceable to something the creator actually does, believes, or has built. Lynn Fisher's annual redesign is a public ten-year practice commitment, not a branding strategy. Devine's monochrome toolkit wiki is a philosophical position about computing longevity, not a design aesthetic. Bruno's RC car is a childhood reference that became a navigation paradigm, not a "creative concept." The lesson repeats across all nine: **mine the subject's own world**.

Three transferable moves recur: (1) **personal-object-as-control** — a toy, self-portrait, or body becomes the literal interactive element; (2) **running motif from real interests** — a growth metaphor, a gaming mechanic, a permacomputing constraint structures the entire IA; (3) **hand-built world instead of template** — the site is built with tools the author also makes, or accumulates logs only they would keep. The fourth move, present in Fisher, MacWright, and Linvega, is **constraint as signature**: a self-imposed rule (one CSS feature per year, one accent color, no redesign) becomes legible over time as the mark of a person who means it.

This is the most important category for any personality skill because it shows the principle at maximum contrast: two developers could use identical tech stacks and produce sites that feel nothing alike, purely because one mined their own world and one imitated someone else's. The slop risk is literal: cloning the RC car, the bird SVGs, or the lil-me cursor follower without generating an equivalent from your own subject produces recognizable imitation rather than personality.
