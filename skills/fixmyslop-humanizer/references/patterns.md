# Humanization behavior families

Use these families to decide where a passage needs revision. Interpret every signal
against genre, audience, and neighboring sentences.

## Mechanical and interface artifacts

Remove pasted chat greetings, closings, emoji decoration, decorative bold, and
heading scaffolding when the text is meant to stand alone. Normalize curly quotes
and reframe em/en dashes in editable prose. Preserve Markdown controls and quoted
interiors when they are part of the requested artifact.

## Formulaic lexicogrammar

Look for stacks of abstract nouns, nominalizations, adjective bundles, participial
explanatory tails, and recurring model-favored bundles. Replace them only when a
plain construction carries the same claim. Do not ban a word globally.

## Rhetorical inflation and promotion

Question claims such as "a pivotal moment", "an enduring testament", "groundbreaking",
or "vibrant" when the source gives no evidence for the added importance. Prefer the
concrete fact. Do not replace a source's stated opinion with a neutral fact or add a
source that was not supplied.

## Templates and redundancy

Check repeated `not X but Y`, staged questions, false `from X to Y` ranges, rule of
three lists, repeated conclusions, and paragraphs that restate the same idea. These
are normal in moderation. Reduce concentration, not every occurrence.

## Distributional monotony

Use the analyzer's sentence templates, sentence-length runs, opening concentration,
paragraph shapes, and cross-segment profiles to find a document that keeps making the
same structural choice. Vary structure only where it improves readability and keeps
the author's register.

## Specificity and context fit

Remove vague attributions and generic implications when the source does not support
them. Do not fabricate names, dates, examples, emotions, anecdotes, or citations.
Preserve legitimate uncertainty, technical precision, dialect, code-switching, and
first-person stance.
