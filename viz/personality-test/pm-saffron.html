A TABLE SET ONCE

Monthly long-table dinners in a private house. A rotating tasting menu built from the Persian pantry — stories told, wine brought, strangers seated together.

Format Long table · BYO wine
Frequency One Friday per month

July 2026 — Tasting Menu

Seven Courses

Hover each course to read its story

  1. 01

    Kashk-e
    Bademjan

    Smoked aubergine with whey

    The aubergine is charred directly on a flame until the skin is black and the flesh collapses into smoke. Folded into kashk and crowned with dried mint — a village dish that arrives before the table has finished its first sentence.

  2. 02

    Ash-e
    Reshteh

    Herb and noodle soup of Nowruz

    Eaten at Persian New Year as an omen — the reshteh noodles represent the threads of fate, and you must break them yourself. Thick with spinach, chickpeas and dried herbs. We serve it in small cups so it opens the table without filling it.

  3. 03

    Mirza
    Ghasemi

    Gilaki smoked aubergine and egg

    Named for a 19th-century governor of Gilan who reportedly dictated the recipe to his cook. Aubergine, garlic, tomato and egg, still in the cast iron it was made in. The story — true or not — arrives with every plate.

  4. 04

    Fesenjan

    Duck with pomegranate and walnut

    One of the oldest Persian preparations, its origins predate written recipe books. Duck slow-braised in a dark sauce of ground walnut and pomegranate molasses, sweet and sour and almost black. The story of Persia in a single bowl.

  5. 05

    Tahdig
    Polo

    Saffron-crusted rice, inverted

    The crust is the prize. A golden disc of saffron-stained rice formed at the base of the pot and brought to the table whole — crackling at the edges, fragrant with bloom. Every Persian family has an argument about who gets the tahdig. It begins here.

  6. 06

    Bastani
    Sonnati

    Saffron ice cream with rosewater

    Iran's original ice cream, thickened with salep orchid flour and stretched with mastic until it holds its shape without a cone. Saffron, rosewater, pistachios. Cold, perfumed, unhurried — a signal that the pace is slowing intentionally.

  7. 07

    Chai-e
    Akhtar

    Safflower tea and Isfahan gaz

    The meal ends as it should — slowly. Akhtar tea, the Persian saffron-safflower blend, poured beside gaz: the pistachio and rosewater nougat made in Isfahan since the Safavid era. The table is allowed to stay as long as it wants. That is the whole point.

The menu rotates entirely each month. Tickets are released the first of the preceding month. BYO wine — we provide the glasses, the stories, and the table.
