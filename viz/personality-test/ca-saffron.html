Course Dish & story Key ingredient
01

Mast-o Khiar

Strained yoghurt, cucumber, dried rose petals, walnuts — a Persian mezze to begin, eaten while the table is still finding its rhythm.

 Rose & walnut
02

Ash-e Reshteh

Herb soup with noodles, kashk, caramelised onion, fenugreek — a soup that has been eaten at the new year and the end of winter for a thousand years.

 Kashk & herb
03

Baghali Polo

Young broad beans, dill, and saffron rice — the dish that marks the arrival of spring in every Persian household. The saffron is steeped for four hours before service.

 Saffron
04

Fesenjan

Slow-braised duck in pomegranate and walnut sauce — a khoresh that takes seven hours and tastes like it. Dark, sour, deeply sweet. The recipe is from the host's grandmother.

 Pomegranate
05

Zereshk Polo

Saffron rice with barberries, crisp-bottom tahdig, served alongside slow-braised lamb shank — the centrepiece. Barberries arrive from the Yazd bazaar twice a year.

 Barberry
06

Halva-ye Ard

Warm wheat halva with saffron, rosewater, and cardamom — a dish that is both dessert and ceremony, traditionally made to mark something important.

 Rosewater
07

Chai & Noghl

Persian black tea brewed long and strong, served with sugar-coated cardamom seeds. The meal ends here, slowly, with no hurry to leave the table.

 Cardamom

Bring your own wine. We provide glasses, water, and the tea. We ask for one bottle per two guests, and no corkage fee — ever. Something from Georgia or the Caucasus always sits well with the food.
